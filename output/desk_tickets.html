One agent versus five

A generalist could inspect a ticket and draft recommendations with fewer handoffs. Specialists help when checks differ: Inventory confirms quantity and vendors, Accounting checks money and prices, Customer Service protects customer wording, and Facilities interprets lease timing. Boss combines findings and requests human action, especially when cash or customer terms are at stake.

The tradeoff is repeated cross-checks, refusals, and ticket 103’s duplicate decision. Attempt/success counts were 11/6, 7/5, and 12/6; the last hit the cap. Specialization pays off when the evidence or approval boundary differs; routine inspections may need only one agent. The orchestrator should assign each fact and decision request a clear owner and reuse cited findings.

The current model records obligations and cash outflows but no sales revenue, refunds, or credits; therefore the approved $417.60 discount is an offer, not a recorded sale. Add sales, refunds, and credits tables and record_sale, issue_refund, and create_store_credit tools. 3. Actually send an approved customer or landlord message. Current drafts are notes; approval does not transmit them. Add a human-gated send_approved_message tool, an outbox table, and a delivery log containing recipient, approved content, sender, timestamps, and delivery result. Customer Service should own customer messages and Facilities should own landlord messages. A human approval click must happen before sending, and the tool should send only the approved version so edits cannot silently bypass review.
